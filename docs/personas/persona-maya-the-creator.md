## The Maya Line — honestly

The direction is right. I already talk like this. "Good. Not £62 good" (E-04.6) is a price statement, not a product statement. You didn't invent my logic, you found it. Fine.

What I'd be proud of: a private rate card. Something that tells me what my word is worth before I sit on a brand call, so I stop pricing myself by vibes.

What makes me wince, hard: **a number next to a brand's name, in public.** I do brand deals. That's a third of my income. The moment The Fence publicly says "MAYA'S PRICE: £40 — sticker £62" about a brand I might work with, I have a legal email and no deal. Nobody in your plan mentions this. It is the single biggest unshipped risk and it's not even flagged.

Concrete fix, cheap: two modes. **Private** shows the number. **Public** shows words only — "she'd buy it in a sale, not at full price" — and honours a contracted-brands blocklist. That blocklist is six brand names. Thirty seconds of my time. Without it I cannot put this anywhere near my bio.

Also: round it, and show a band. "£45–£55." Precision I don't possess reads as a machine wearing my face.

And your held-out test is weaker than you think. Deleting the Glass Drop note and still landing low might just mean the model has a generic "expensive dewy serum is suss" prior — the exact failure you already hit in Attempt 1. Test me on an **asymmetry**: I'll defend SPF at £60 and refuse a £34 cleanser. If it reproduces that, I believe you.
