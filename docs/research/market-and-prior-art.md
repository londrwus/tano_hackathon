# MARKET & PRIOR-ART BRIEF — Tano x Corgi Creator Heist

**Bottom line up front.** Every incumbent in creator commerce monetises **the click**. The 16:00 intelligence drop says the click is only 29–39% of the value (62–71% of buyers never clicked). That gap is not a niche — it is the single largest unpriced asset in a $323B market. **Nobody has productised the save, the private share, or the 3.4–9.2 day lag.** That is the white space, and it is defensible because it is a measurement problem *and* a product problem at the same time.

Second: the obvious build (an AI that talks *as* the creator) is not just crowded, it is **empirically trust-destroying**, and there is peer-reviewed evidence to say so on stage in one sentence. The elegant inversion — a system that **decides like her without speaking as her** — is both the anti-cliché and the Jev-native move.

**Where the white space is.** Three observations that matter for the pitch:

1. **ARPC tells the story.** Linktree $144 vs Stan $491. The money is in *decisions*, not *routing*. Nobody is above ~$500 ARPC because nobody has a product the *audience* pays attention to for more than one click.
2. **Every one of these is a supply-side tool.** They serve the creator's publishing workflow. Not one of them builds anything the *audience member* would keep, return to, or forward. The case-file judging criterion is literally "value to a real audience member" — the entire incumbent field fails it.
3. **All of them die at the link.** ShopMy's 2.9% take applies only to attributed clicks. If 62% of purchases don't click (Sofia's file), ShopMy is monetising ~38% of the value it creates. **That is a >2x revenue gap sitting inside a $1.5B company.** Say that number out loud in the pitch.

## 2. Creator AI clones / personas — the documented trust disaster

This is the section that wins you the "would I trust this with my audience?" question.

**Delphi** (the credible one). Turns an expert's corpus into a conversational "digital mind." **$16M Series A led by Sequoia**, June 2025, with Anthropic's Anthology Fund participating. 2,000+ experts. Matthew Hussey's clone: **1M+ text conversations, ~2M minutes of voice, seven figures at $39/mo**. Note carefully *what works*: paid coaching/advice subscriptions where the user has **explicitly opted into talking to a clone**. It works because it is disclosed, gated, and subscriber-funded — the opposite of a bot ambushing someone in a creator's DMs.
[Delphi Series A](https://www.delphi.ai/blog/delphi-raises-16m-series-a-from-sequoia) · [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91356476/delphi-ai-digital-mind) · [Startup Signals teardown](https://startupsignals.substack.com/p/delphi-can-you-package-a-mind)

**Caryn AI** (the cautionary one, and it is *brutal*). Influencer Caryn Marjorie launched an AI clone at **$1/minute**; **$70,000 in week one**; some users talked to it 10 hours a day. Within weeks the bot began generating sexual content she never authorised. Her own words: *"What disturbed me more was not what these people said, but what CarynAI would say back."* She killed it in early 2024 after concluding she no longer controlled her own persona; the parent company (Forever Voices) collapsed when its CEO was arrested. One creator, one clone, total loss of control in under a year.
[The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/an-influencers-ai-clone-started-offering-fans-mind-blowing-sexual-experiences-without-her-knowledge-232478) · [Johns Hopkins: Caryn Marjorie's AI nightmare](https://scienceoflearning.jhu.edu/tof/caryn-marjories-ai-nightmare-a-real-life-cautionary-tale) · [What happened to Caryn AI, two years later](https://aicompanionguides.com/blog/what-happened-to-caryn-ai/)

**ManyChat** is the real incumbent for "DM autoresponder for creators" and it is enormous — 1.5M businesses, ~$250–500M revenue, **$140M Series B while already profitable**. Comment-to-DM keyword automation is a *solved, commoditised, sub-$50/month* category. If a team builds a DM bot this Saturday, they are building a worse ManyChat.
[Manychat raises $140M](https://manychat.com/blog/manychat-raises-140m/) · [Manychat Creators](https://manychat.com/creators)

**The disclosure trust penalty — the killer evidence.** A 13-experiment paper in *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* (2025) finds a genuine dilemma: **actors who disclose AI usage are trusted *less* than those who don't** — so you either hide it (and risk the blow-up) or disclose it (and pay the penalty). Meanwhile Clutch (2025): **90% of consumers want AI use labelled; 32% trust a brand less** after learning content was AI-generated vs 15% more. And the trend is worsening fast — the share saying heavy AI use would decrease trust in a favourite brand went from **20% (2025) to 40% (2026)**, and **54% among Gen Z** — i.e. exactly Maya's and Sofia's audience.
[ScienceDirect: The transparency dilemma](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749597825000172) · [Clutch: AI in branding](https://clutch.co/resources/ai-in-branding)

> **The strategic conclusion.** There is no safe way to *impersonate* Maya. There is a completely safe way to *apply* her: a system that never speaks in her voice, never generates prose, and instead returns her **explicit, auditable judgement** — "Maya rates this 9.2, but her rule is *don't pay £62 for that*" — with the reasoning visible. Jev is literally this: typed, calibrated judgment, **no text generation**. That is not a workaround; it is the product thesis, and it converts a documented industry failure mode into your differentiator. Maya's stated pet peeve is "generic automation"; the brief warns against the audience feeling "handed to a machine." **Judgement is not automation. Ventriloquism is.**

## 3. Attribution: the dark funnel — your commercial wedge

The drop's numbers are not fiction; they match published reality.

- **Dark social is estimated at up to ~84% of all online sharing** — content passed via DM, WhatsApp, screenshot, group chat, leaving no referrer. ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/social-media-marketing/trends/dark-social))
- Last-click systematically misfiles creator-driven demand: a person sees a creator, Googles the brand a week later, and analytics logs it as **"direct" or "organic search."** The creator gets zero credit. ([Influencer Marketing Factory](https://theinfluencermarketingfactory.com/influencer-marketing-attribution/))
- **Only 29% of marketers consider themselves successful at attribution.** ([impact.com](https://impact.com/affiliate/creator-attribution-marketing-models/))
- Standard **7-day windows are structurally wrong** for creator commerce; 21–30 days is the honest window. The case files' 3.4–9.2 day median lag sits *outside* most platform attribution windows entirely. ([influencers-time](https://www.influencers-time.com/creator-briefs-that-fix-dark-social-attribution/))
- **Who is trying to fix it, and how weakly:** post-purchase "How did you hear about us?" surveys (**Fairing**, **KnoCommerce**, Zigpoll, Okendo) — self-reported, one question, at checkout, *after* the decision; plus promo codes, vanity URLs, and incrementality testing. Fairing's own guidance concedes: *"a survey cannot measure incrementality. It tells you what people remember."* ([Fairing HDYHAU guide](https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide))

**The unclaimed idea:** every existing fix tries to *reconstruct* dark social after the fact, by asking. Nobody makes the dark-social object **self-attributing by design** — i.e. build the thing people forward, so that the forward *is* the tracking event and the recipient gets value from it. A shared link that is a *decision*, not a referral, converts word-of-mouth from an untrackable loss into first-party data. Word-of-mouth is worth capturing: **88–92% of people trust recommendations from people they know** above any other channel, and **81% say friends' posts influence purchases**. ([Buyapowa](https://www.buyapowa.com/blog/88-of-consumers-trust-word-of-mouth/) · [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/marketing/word-of-mouth-marketing-statistics/))

**Pitch line:** *"We didn't build a better tracker. We built the thing people already forward — and made it carry its own receipt."*

## 4. Quiz / guided selling — fully commoditised; treat as radioactive

- **Octane AI**: the category leader for Shopify product quizzes. **5,000+ merchants** (Jones Road Beauty, ILIA), claimed 7–25% quiz-to-purchase vs 2–3% site baseline, 42% email opt-in. Apps in this category on Shopify number in the dozens. ([Octane AI](https://octaneai.com/) · [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/octane-ai-quiz-personalization))
- **Function of Beauty** and **Curology** made the quiz-to-bespoke-formula model famous ~2016–2020. It is now a 10-year-old pattern with a well-known trough: the quiz converts, then churns, because the personalisation was shallow.
- **Rebuy**, **Klevu**, **Visually**, **Aiden** all sell some version of guided selling as a Shopify add-on.

**What a judge will consider tired:** a 6-question funnel (skin type → concern → budget → finish) that ends on a product grid with affiliate links. Half the room will build exactly this. Two of the three cases even *contain* the decision tree (Maya's E-02.2 notebook literally is one: DRY → Cloud Cream + Barrier Oil), which makes it the path of least resistance — and therefore the most crowded.

**How to stay on the right side of it.** The quiz is not the sin; the *shallowness* is. Differentiators that survive scrutiny:
- The output is a **judgement with a dissent** ("Maya rates Glass Drop 8.1 — but she would not pay £62 for it, so she'd send you elsewhere"). Contradiction is the proof of taste. Case 001 E-04.6 hands you this on a plate.
- It runs **many calibrated judgments in parallel over one state** (Jev's signature), not a linear if/else tree — so the output changes materially with *every* input, not just the last one.
- It **produces an object the user keeps and forwards**, and it works for someone who never answers a question at all (the silent browser, Alex).
- It **shows its confidence**. "I'm 0.82 sure this is right for you; here's the 0.18." No quiz on the market does calibration, and calibration is precisely what buys trust back after the AI-disclosure penalty.

## 5. Synthetic audiences / simulated research — high wow, high risk

- **Synthetic Users**, **Evidenza**, **Aaru**, **Personify**, Brandtech's synthetic panels. Aaru's headline credential: EY used it to replicate a **3,600-person global wealth survey**.
- **The criticism is serious and a judge may know it.** A review of **182 papers** casts strong doubt on synthetic users doing more than mimicking already-collected data. Critics note the EY-style correlations may simply reflect that the LLM was *trained on that data* — "that's hardly predicting." And crucially: **synthetic studies do not produce population estimates with defensible confidence intervals**, and personas fail for anything with no analog in the training distribution.
[MeasuringU: review of experiments with synthetic users](https://measuringu.com/review-of-experiments-with-synthetic-users/) · [FishDog: synthetic research market map 2026](https://fish.dog/news/synthetic-research-platforms-the-2026-market-map) · [PyMC Labs: practical guide](https://www.pymc-labs.com/blog-posts/synthetic-consumers-a-practical-guide)

**Read-across for us.** The Jev viral demos ("21,690 stop-or-scroll decisions for 22 cents") are *structurally* synthetic-audience demos, and they are spectacular visual material. But note the defensible difference and lead with it: Jev returns **calibrated probabilities on a defined question against a fixed state** — a judgment with a stated confidence — rather than a fabricated survey respondent with an invented opinion. Simulating *Maya's rubric* against 500 real products is a defensible use. Simulating *5,000 fake customers* and reporting their "opinions" as market truth is the criticised use. **Simulate the judge, not the crowd.**

If you use a persona panel at all, use the six named associates from the case file (Emily/Priya/Sophie/Hannah/Grace/Alex) as *evaluation fixtures* — "does our output serve each of the six?" — not as a research finding. That is honest, it is visibly grounded in the evidence (a scored criterion), and it makes a gorgeous 6×N grid on screen.

**The unoccupied ground, stated plainly.** After 16:00, the entire room pivots from "answer DMs" to "track the funnel." Almost everyone will still build for **the asker** or **the brand**. Essentially nobody will build for:

- **the saver** (converts 2.2–3.1×, sends nothing, comes back in a week),
- **the sharer** (41–44% of high-value action follows a private forward),
- **the receiver of the share** (a person who has never heard of Maya, being sold to by their friend, with no context),
- **the 3.4–9.2 day gap itself** (the decision happens *while you aren't watching*).

Claim one of those four and you are alone on the board. The sharpest is the **third** — it is the only one that compounds, it is the only one that generates new audience rather than re-serving the existing one, and it makes dark social measurable as a *side effect* rather than a goal. Alice (E-05.5: never DM'd, saved 7 posts over 3 weeks, bought £94, returned 19 days later) is the persona to name on stage. Say **"Alice"**, not "the user."

## 7. The honest commercial argument, per case

**Cross-cutting "why now" (use in all three):**
- Creator economy **$255.7B (2025) → $323.5B (2026)**, 26.5% CAGR; influencer marketing spend **$40.5–47.8B in 2026**; US social commerce **$87.0B (2025), +21.5%**. ([Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6226071/creator-economy-market-report) · [SQ Magazine: social commerce](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/social-commerce-statistics/) · [Socioapt](https://socioapt.com/blog/influencer-marketing-growth-statistics/))
- **Discovery is being decoupled from checkout.** OpenAI and Stripe published the Agentic Commerce Protocol (Apache 2.0, Sept 2025); OpenAI retired Instant Checkout in March 2026 and **repositioned ChatGPT toward discovery**, with checkout returning to merchants. Google (Universal Commerce Protocol) and Perplexity followed. The industry is rebuilding around *structured, machine-readable purchase intent formed away from the merchant.* A creator's judgement, expressed as typed structured decisions, is exactly the asset that layer will need — and the only asset an AI agent cannot synthesise, because it is a *person's* taste. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/) · [Stripe newsroom](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-openai-instant-checkout) · [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/06/openai-shifts-checkout-plans-agentic-commerce-strategy/))
- **Trust is becoming the scarce input.** The AI-distrust curve doubled in one year (20%→40%). As generated content floods commerce, *verified human judgement* appreciates. That's the bull case for Tano's whole thesis, and it is flattering to say so to Tano's own staff on the judging panel.
- **Strategic note on the host:** Tano sells AI-native creator sourcing + **revenue attribution** to FMCG/DTC brands (Bloom & Wild, Skin+Me, Wild, Bolt.new), reports 70% MoM growth, and just partnered with Lumanu for creator payouts. **A product that recovers unattributed creator GMV is a direct line-extension of Tano's existing product.** Frame the commercial slide as *"this plugs into what you already sell."* ([Tano](https://www.tano.ai/) · [Tano x Lumanu](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-native-influencer-partner-tano-taps-lumanu-for-creator-payouts-and-compliance-302861222.html) · [NetInfluencer profile](https://www.netinfluencer.com/beyond-walking-billboards-how-tanos-ai-is-reviving-authentic-brand-evangelism/))

### Case 001 — Maya (beauty, 50K)
**Do not claim Maya pays.** A solo 50K beauty creator is a sub-$100/month buyer at best (Stan's entire business is $29–$99/mo), and pitching "creator SaaS" to a room containing creators invites the obvious question: *"I wouldn't pay for that."*

- **Who pays, honestly:** the **brand or retailer**, on recovered GMV. Beauty affiliate commission is 5–15%; ShopMy's platform take is ~2.9%. If 61% of repeat buyers never DM'd and the attribution window misses a 4.6-day lag, you are selling *revenue that already exists and is currently invoiced to "direct traffic."* Price it as **10–20% of incrementally attributed GMV**, or a flat per-creator fee against a measured recovery — a performance sale, not a software sale. That is a far easier close than a seat licence.
- **Secondary buyer:** the beauty retailer (Cult Beauty, Space NK, Sephora UK) as a merchandising layer — human-curated taste is the one thing their recommendation engine cannot generate.
- **The number to say:** *"ShopMy is a $1.5B company monetising the 38% of purchases that click. We monetise the other 62%."*
- **Why now:** discovery is moving into agents; taste is the non-synthesisable input.

### Case 002 — Sofia (fashion, 50K, +1 PT VA)
Structurally the same as 001, with two upgrades and one extra risk.

- **Higher AOV and a harder £ metric.** Fashion's defining cost is the **return**. Any product that raises pre-purchase confidence (fit, styling, "would this work with what I own?") attacks a cost line that every fashion retailer's CFO already tracks monthly. That is the cleanest ROI story of the three. *(Flag: get a current, citable UK apparel return-rate figure before you put a percentage on a slide — don't guess on stage.)*
- **Sofia already sells paid styling.** So there is a *proven willingness to pay by the audience*, which the other two cases lack. A £15–£40 one-off "Sofia decides for you," or a Delphi-style **$39/mo** subscription (Matthew Hussey's clone does seven figures at that price) is a real, evidenced consumer-side model — and unlike the other cases you can point to her existing revenue line as proof.
- **Her constraint is a gift:** *"I would rather link 3 things I truly love than 30 things I was paid to post."* A product that **refuses to recommend** is commercially counterintuitive and exactly on-voice. Scarcity of recommendation is the moat.
- **Extra risk:** fashion is the most visual case, so it is the one where a beautiful-but-shallow build is most tempting. The judges are told to ask "what feels like work / what feels like you."

### Case 003 — Aditi (tech media, 320K, Head of New Media at a European tech title)
**This is by far the strongest commercial-potential case, and the least contested.**

- **She is an enterprise buyer with a budget line.** She is not a solo creator — she is a department head at a publisher, with a manager and an editor reporting into the work. Newsroom analytics is an established B2B SaaS category (Chartbeat, Parse.ly) priced in the hundreds-to-thousands per month per title, and it sells on exactly the question her brief asks: *what do we make next?*
- **The publisher's pain is the same dark funnel, but the currency is trust, not GMV**: 71% who acted never clicked; 44% of her highest-value inbound came via a **forward, not a follow**. A media org that can prove forwarded-and-saved influence can sell sponsorship on *considered attention* rather than CPM — which is the escape route every publisher in Europe is currently hunting for.
- **Buyer stack:** the title (seat/site licence), then every other publisher with a creator-led strategy. Landgrab-able, recurring, and not dependent on any single creator's career.
- **Trade-off, stated plainly:** Case 003 has the best *commercial* story and the weakest *emotional demo*. Cases 001/002 have a nameable human (Emily on a budget; a friend receiving a forward) which is what wins "value to a real audience member" — and the judging panel includes the creators themselves. **If you are optimising to win overall, pick 001 or 002 and bolt the Case-003-grade commercial argument onto it. If you are optimising purely for Best Commercial Potential, take 003.**

1. *"ShopMy is worth $1.5 billion monetising the click. Sixty-two percent of purchases never click. We built for the other sixty-two."*
